Our Story

From the South Side
to the South Platte.

A sandwich worth moving 1,000 miles for.

Jimmy grew up in a Chicago neighborhood where the corner beef stand wasn't just lunch — it was tradition. Thin-sliced roast beef, piled high on a sturdy Italian roll, dunked in seasoned jus, topped with sweet peppers or fiery giardiniera. That was the after-school ritual. That was Sunday after church. That was home.

When Jimmy relocated to Denver, he loved the mountains, the green chile, and the wide-open skies. What he couldn't find? A proper Italian beef. Not one. So he did what any stubborn Chicagoan would do: he built it himself.

Small truck, big standards.

Everything on the truck is made the way Jimmy's grandmother would've approved of:

  • Top round roasted low and slow — overnight, every night.
  • Vienna Beef franks and Polish sausages, shipped in from Chicago.
  • Giardiniera made in-house, so we can make it spicy.
  • Sturdy Italian rolls sourced from a local Denver bakery — the only "local" shortcut we take, and it's a good one.
  • Sport peppers, yellow mustard, neon relish, tomato, pickle, celery salt. Never ketchup. Never.
